PROJECT: Evidence Collection for AI-Powered Photo Retrieval

CONTEXT

We are researching a product problem related to photo retrieval in Google Photos and similar photo-management applications.

The hypothesis being investigated is:

"Users have difficulty retrieving specific photos from their large photo libraries, and an AI-powered natural-language search experience could make photo retrieval easier than relying only on traditional search, albums, dates, locations, objects, or automatically detected faces."

IMPORTANT:
This project is currently in the EVIDENCE COLLECTION stage.

Do NOT assume that the hypothesis is true.
Do NOT search only for reviews that support the hypothesis.
We need both positive and negative evidence.

The goal is to identify what real users say about:
1. Difficulty finding/retrieving photos.
2. Searching for photos using natural language or descriptive queries.
3. Problems with existing Google Photos search.
4. Problems with face/person recognition.
5. Problems with object/content recognition.
6. Problems with date/location-based retrieval.
7. Users' expectations from AI-powered photo search.
8. Cases where users cannot find a photo even though they know it exists.
9. Cases where AI/photo search successfully helps users find something.
10. Cases where AI search gives irrelevant, incorrect, incomplete, or unexpected results.

PRIMARY SOURCE

Use Google Play Store reviews, especially reviews of:
- Google Photos
- Other major photo-management/photo-gallery applications when relevant

Google Photos should be the primary source because this is the product being studied.

Do NOT treat every review mentioning "search" as relevant.
The review must contain evidence related to photo discovery, photo retrieval, photo organization, search, recognition, or finding specific photos.

--------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: COLLECT RAW REVIEWS
--------------------------------------------------

Collect as many relevant Google Play Store reviews as possible.

For every review that passes the broad relevance filter, preserve:

- Review text exactly as written
- App name
- Review date, if available
- Star rating, if available
- Reviewer name/ID, if available and permitted
- Any available metadata
- Source URL, if available

DO NOT paraphrase the original review during collection.

The original wording is important because this dataset will be used as research evidence.

--------------------------------------------------
STEP 4: IMPORTANT FILTERING PRINCIPLE
--------------------------------------------------

DO NOT FILTER BASED ONLY ON KEYWORDS.

A review does NOT need to contain words such as:
"search", "AI", "find", "photo search", or "retrieval".

The semantic meaning of the review matters.

For example:

"Every time I want to show someone an old picture, I have to scroll forever."

KEEP.

This is evidence of a retrieval/discovery problem even though the word "search" may not appear.

Another example:

"I wish I could just type 'photos from my Goa trip with my friends' and get them."

KEEP.

Another example:

"The app is great and I purchased 2TB."

REMOVE unless there is additional evidence about retrieval/search.

--------------------------------------------------
STEP 6: CLASSIFY THE DIRECTION OF EVIDENCE
--------------------------------------------------

For every included review, classify the evidence as one of:

PAIN_POINT
- User describes a problem, frustration, failure, or unmet need.

FEATURE_REQUEST
- User explicitly asks for a capability.

SUCCESS
- User describes successful retrieval/search.

FAILURE
- User describes unsuccessful or inaccurate retrieval/search.

EXPECTATION
- User describes what they expect the product to understand or do.

MIXED
- Review contains both positive and negative evidence.

Do NOT convert these into subjective judgments about the product.

--------------------------------------------------
STEP 11: DETECT STRONG EVIDENCE
--------------------------------------------------

Flag reviews as HIGH_SIGNAL when they contain specific behavioral evidence.

Examples:

HIGH_SIGNAL:
"I searched for photos of my daughter at her school function and it only showed 3 pictures even though I have dozens."

HIGH_SIGNAL:
"I have over 20,000 photos and finding an old picture means scrolling for hours."

HIGH_SIGNAL:
"I wish I could type what I remember about a picture instead of knowing the exact date."

HIGH_SIGNAL:
"The face recognition doesn't identify my father in photos where he is looking away."

LOW_SIGNAL:
"Search could be better."

The goal is to prioritize evidence containing a concrete user behavior, task, problem, failure, or expectation.

--------------------------------------------------
STEP 12: DO NOT CREATE FALSE EVIDENCE
--------------------------------------------------

CRITICAL RULE:

Never fabricate, rewrite, merge, or embellish reviews.

Never turn a vague review into a specific user problem.

Never assume that a user wants AI search unless the review provides evidence supporting that interpretation.

Never infer demographic information unless explicitly stated.

Never infer intent that is not reasonably supported by the review.

If uncertain, mark the field as UNKNOWN.

--------------------------------------------------
STEP 13: REMOVE DUPLICATES
--------------------------------------------------

If the same review appears multiple times:

- Keep one copy.
- Preserve the most complete version.
- Record that duplicates were detected if metadata allows.

Do not artificially increase evidence counts by counting duplicated reviews.

STEP 15: CREATE A RESEARCH SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------

After filtering and classification, generate a separate summary.

The summary MUST NOT simply count keyword mentions.

Instead, identify patterns in actual user behavior.

Report:

1. Total reviews collected
2. Total relevant reviews
3. Total excluded reviews
4. Number of high-signal reviews
5. Number of reviews describing retrieval difficulty
6. Number describing search failure
7. Number describing successful retrieval
8. Number requesting better search
9. Number related to face/person recognition
10. Number related to object/content recognition
11. Number related to natural-language/AI search
12. Number related to large photo libraries

Then identify recurring patterns such as:

- Users knowing a photo exists but being unable to find it.
- Users relying on manual scrolling.
- Users forgetting the exact date/location.
- Search returning irrelevant results.
- Face recognition failing in specific visual conditions.
- Users wanting to describe what they remember rather than use metadata.
- Users successfully finding photos through semantic/natural-language search.

Only report a pattern if supported by multiple reviews or particularly strong specific evidence.

--------------------------------------------------
STEP 16: SEPARATE EVIDENCE FROM INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------

The final output must clearly distinguish:

RAW USER EVIDENCE
from
RESEARCH INTERPRETATION.

Do not state:

"Users definitely need AI photo search."

Instead state:

"X reviews describe difficulty retrieving photos using existing mechanisms."

Then show the underlying evidence.

Do not convert evidence into a product conclusion prematurely.

--------------------------------------------------
FINAL RESEARCH OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------

The purpose of this dataset is NOT to prove that an AI-powered photo retrieval system is the correct solution.

The purpose is to answer these questions:

1. Do users actually experience difficulty retrieving photos from large libraries?
2. What specific retrieval tasks are difficult?
3. What methods do users currently use?
4. Where do existing search/retrieval mechanisms fail?
5. What do users explicitly ask for?
6. Are there examples of users successfully using natural-language/semantic/AI search?
7. What recurring unmet needs can be supported by actual user evidence?
8. What evidence contradicts or weakens the hypothesis?

The final research output should allow a product team to move from:

USER REVIEW
→ USER BEHAVIOR
→ RETRIEVAL PROBLEM
→ PATTERN
→ EVIDENCE-BACKED OPPORTUNITY

without skipping the evidence stage.

IMPORTANT FINAL RULE:

Do not optimize the dataset to support the proposed AI solution.

If the reviews indicate that the real problem is something else, report that honestly.

Evidence should determine the problem, and the problem should determine whether an AI solution is justified.
